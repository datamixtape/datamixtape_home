Do you have an aunt in Virginia who's oddly obsessed with the Iowa senate
elections? A nephew in Arizona who can't stop talking about Sherrod Brown? Are
these isolated incidents, or do donors in certain areas have a predilection for
inserting themselves into the political happenings of specific states?
That's the idea behind [the Political Donor Map](/data/donor-map): Understanding
where a given state's political donations tend to go, and where the incoming cash
tends to come from.

The map can be filtered down to each state, the direction of the money flow
(sends/receives), political party (Dem/Rep/All), and unit being measured (dollars
vs. donors). States tend to over-index on flows to and from neighboring states,
and so those states can be removed from the model and analysis with the click of
a button.

Because the raw totals tend to be boring (California is the biggest out of state
Democratic donor for most states), the map defaults to showing the flows in and
out of each state compared to the nation as a whole. If Montana gives Kentucky a
bigger share of its out-of-state money than the rest of the country, that line is
green. If it gives less, it's red. You can play around with the embedded map
below.

## Friends of Amy McGrath

Part of the fun is finding the small, strange flows. Montana's Democratic money
goes to Kentucky at 6.2 times the national rate. That's 17 Montanans, and 14 of
them gave the $3,500 maximum to Amy McGrath's Senate campaign. Lots of McGrath
friends in Montana!

## Feeling the Bern in Alaska

Alaska sends Vermont 3.1 times its expected share of Democratic money. That's 101
Alaskans giving to Bernie Sanders, who the map counts with the Democrats. Oregon
is right behind at 2.9 times, with more than 600 donors.

## Next door

The most lopsided pair in the country is Missouri to Kansas: more than 23 times
the national pattern for Democratic money. That's Kansas City, which straddles the
state line. Lots of pairs of neighbors look like this, which is why there's a
**Hide neighboring states** button.

## Most wanted

Per resident, no state draws more out-of-state money than Maine, where candidates
have taken in $11.52 for every person who lives there. Set the map to **All** and
clear the state to see the full ranking at the bottom of the page.
