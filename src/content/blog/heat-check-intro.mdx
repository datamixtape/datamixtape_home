Every NBA fan has a theory about how their team performs when certain players are
on the court. Some of them even have the data to back it up, wielding ORtgs, DRtgs,
rim and 3PtA percentages to slay bad arguments from AI slop bots. But wouldn't it be nice to visualize exactly what's
happening -- which shots are being taken -- with any combination of players on the
floor? [Heat Check](/data/heat-check) aims to answer that question with shot
heatmaps for every team, with any combination of players on or off the court, for
offense and defense.

Take a look at the heat map below, which shows the frequency of Knicks field goal
attempts last year with Brunson on the court vs. off the court. You can see the
increased volume in Brunson's favorite spots near the elbows.

So how does Heat Check work? Pick any team and then choose a lineup by toggling any
players to be On or Off court. You can choose as many as five players to be on
court, and you can leave players in as neither on nor off, which will calculate
shot metrics based only on players on or off. Each lineup will then have a
comparison lineup that can be composed of players from the same team or different
teams. If you want to compare Spurs Wemby lineups to Spurs non-Wemby lineups or to
Wolves Gobert lineups, you can do it. You can also compare to the league average
lineup.

There are additional toggles for regular season and playoffs, date ranges, or
specific opponents. That lets you compare Spurs Wemby lineups against the Nuggets
to Spurs Wemby lineups against OKC (beware of small sample sizes).

The visualization defaults to shot volume, but you can also toggle to view shot
accuracy from any part of the court. Hover over any zone for the specific
percentages.
