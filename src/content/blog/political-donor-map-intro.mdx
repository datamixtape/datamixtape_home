A retiree in Oregon sends $50 to a Senate race in Vermont. A lawyer in Manhattan
maxes out to a House candidate in Georgia. Add it all up and more than half a
billion dollars in House and Senate donations has crossed a state line this
cycle. That's the idea behind [the Political Donor Map](/data/donor-map): where
does each state's political money go, and where does it come from?

The raw totals are a little boring. California sends the most money to almost
everyone, and California to New York ($9.4 million) is the biggest flow in the
country. So the map compares every pair of states to the national pattern. If
Montana gives Kentucky a bigger share of its out-of-state money than the rest of
the country does, that line is green. If it gives less, it's red. The darker the
line, the bigger the gap.

Pick a state, then choose **Sends** to see where its donors' money goes or
**Receives** to see where its candidates' money comes from. You can also look at
just Democratic or Republican money, and switch between dollars and donors. The
map opens on Alaska's Democratic candidates, who get 2.6 times more of their
out-of-state money from Washington than the national pattern predicts.

## Friends of Amy McGrath

Part of the fun is finding the small, strange flows. Montana's Democratic money
goes to Kentucky at 6.3 times the national rate. That's 17 Montanans, and 14 of
them gave the $3,500 maximum to Amy McGrath's Senate campaign. Lots of McGrath
friends in Montana!

## Feeling the Bern in Alaska

Alaska sends Vermont 3.1 times its expected share of Democratic money. That's 101
Alaskans giving to Bernie Sanders, who the map counts with the Democrats. Oregon
is right behind at 2.9 times, with more than 600 donors.

## Next door

The most lopsided pair in the country is Missouri to Kansas: almost 24 times the
national pattern for Democratic money. That's Kansas City, which straddles the
state line. Lots of pairs of neighbors look like this, which is why there's a
**Hide neighboring states** button.

## Most wanted

Per resident, no state draws more out-of-state money than Maine, where candidates
have taken in $11.52 for every person who lives there. Set the map to **All** and
clear the state to see the full ranking at the bottom of the page.
