Every NBA fan has a theory about what one player does to their team. The offense
gets stagnant when the point guard sits. Opponents stop attacking the rim when the
big man is in.
The shooters go cold without the star drawing attention. Some of those theories
are right and some of them are just vibes, but the play-by-play can tell you which
is which. That's the idea behind [Heat Check](/data/heat-check): Shot heatmaps for
any NBA player or five-man lineup, on the floor or off it, on offense or defense.

Pick a team and a lineup, then compare it to the league average or to a custom
lineup of your own (the most useful one is usually the same team with that player
on the bench). The map toggles between **Volume**, which shows where the shots come
from, and **Accuracy**, which shows how often they go in. Hover over any zone for
the numbers. It covers every team for the 2025-26 season, regular season and
playoffs.

The embedded map below opens on the Knicks with Jalen Brunson on the floor versus
the Knicks with him on the bench. Look at the midrange: with Brunson playing, 13.2%
of New York's shots come from midrange, and without him it's 8.2%. That's the
fourth-biggest jump of any regular in the league.

## Nobody drives on Jaren Jackson Jr.

Flip the map to defense and some players just make the rim disappear. With Jaren
Jackson Jr. on the floor this season, 23.5% of Memphis's opponents' shots came at
the rim. With him on the bench, 33.5%. That 10-point drop is the biggest in the
league, ahead of Donovan Clingan (8.0) and well ahead of the usual suspects, Rudy
Gobert (5.6) and Victor Wembanyama (4.9). Teams don't seem to want to find out
what happens when they challenge him.
